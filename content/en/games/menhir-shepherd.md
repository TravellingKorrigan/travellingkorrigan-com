Old Breton tales say that on certain nights the menhirs leave their places and go down to the water to drink, then come back before anyone sees. Tonight the stones have wandered off like sheep, and the korrigan herds them home. He pushes each stone back into its socket, one at a time, and can never pull. When the last stone is home, the sun rises over the field. Then the next night.

## 128 nights in four regions

The first nights, the alignments of Carnac, the shore of the Gulf of Morbihan at Locmariaquer, and the clearings of the forest of Brocéliande. Each region has its own ground, its own night sounds and its own dawn: the wind on the heath, the sea lapping and a far bell buoy on the gulf, the leaves and an owl in the forest. Every night is open from the start, and a small sun marks the ones you have solved.

## Two korrigans

Play as the korriganez in her lace coiffe, or as the korrigan in his round Breton hat. Both carry a small lantern, push with all their weight, and rest against a menhir when the night is done.

## Made to be calm

There is no timer and no score. Undo is unlimited, one push at a time, and a night starts again in one tap. When a stone can no longer go home, the korrigan scratches his head, and Step back takes you to the last moment the night could still be solved. The game never says you are stuck when you are not.

Each night is tagged Gentle, Classic or Tricky, and each region is sorted from the easiest night to the hardest. Nothing is locked.

On a phone, an arrow pad of carved stones moves the korrigan, and you can also tap a square to walk there or tap a stone to see where it can go. A tiny vibration marks each step, and goes off with the sound.
